Precious Metals Price Forecast

ARIMA forecasts for gold, silver, platinum and palladium, and how good they really are

Enrique Pérez Herrero

10 November 2026

What this is

  • A small data product from the Coursera course Developing Data Products (2015), rebuilt in 2026 with Quarto.
  • It downloads five years of daily prices of the four precious metals and forecasts the next 63 trading days (about three months) with auto.arima() from the forecast package.
  • It also checks the forecasts against a naive one (tomorrow = today), which is the honest benchmark for any asset price.
  • Companion Shiny app: Precious-Metals-Prediction.

Data up to 09 November 2026. Not investment advice.

Is ARIMA better than doing nothing?

Metal Model RMSE ARIMA RMSE naive MAPE ARIMA (%) MAPE naive (%)
Gold ARIMA(0,1,0) with drift 363.13 250.90 7.17 4.92
Silver ARIMA(0,1,1) 6.67 6.66 8.85 8.85
Platinum ARIMA(1,1,0) 128.30 128.34 6.25 6.25
Palladium ARIMA(3,1,2) 200.44 207.40 14.22 14.88

Each model is fitted leaving out the last 63 days and compared with what really happened. ARIMA beats the naive forecast in 2 of 4 metals. Prices behave almost like a random walk: the useful output is the width of the bands (the risk), not the central line.

The model, step by step

  1. Logarithm of the price: changes become percentages.
  2. Differences: auto.arima() tests for unit roots (KPSS) and almost always takes one difference, the daily return.
  3. Orders p and q chosen by AICc.
  4. Residuals: should look like white noise (Ljung–Box test, right).
Metal Model AICc Ljung-Box p
Gold ARIMA(0,1,0) with drift -7522.922 0.217
Silver ARIMA(0,1,1) -5648.873 0.004
Platinum ARIMA(1,1,0) -5928.464 0.092
Palladium ARIMA(1,1,3) -5284.956 0.661

A p-value above 0.05 means no autocorrelation is left in the residuals. Volatility clusters (calm and stormy periods) remain: that is a job for GARCH models, not ARIMA.
